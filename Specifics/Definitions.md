### Lifecycle
- **Application:** The borrower's formal request for a mortgage
- **Origination:** The process of creating a new mortgage
- **Closing:** The final step, where all the documents are signed; the loan officially exists
- **Pull-through rate:** The fraction of applications that end up closing.
- **Fallout:** When the application doesn't close
	- **Denied:** The lender rejected it (too much debt, weak credit, etc.)
	- **Withdrawn:** The borrower walked away
	- **Incomplete:** Borrower never finished providing the required documents
### Types of Loans
- **Purchase Loan:** A mortgage used to buy a home: more labor intensive as it requires a sale to coordinate, appraisal, inspection, deadline, etc.
- **Refinance:** Replacing an existing mortgage with a new one on the same house, usually to get a lower rate
- **Conventional Loan:** A mortgage not  insured by the government, usually bought from Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac
- **FHA Loan:** Insured by the Federal Housing Administration: allows down payments as low as 3.5% and lower credit scores, but requires more paperwork
- **VA Loan:** Guaranteed by the department of Veteran Affairs for eligible service members
- **30-year fixed rate mortgage:** The standard US mortgage: one interest rate for the full 30 years with equal monthly payments
### Borrower and Loan Characteristics
- **Loan Amount:** The amount borrowed, i.e., the home price minus the down payment
- **Debt-to-income ratio (DTI):** The borrower's total monthly debt payments (mortgage, car, student loans, credit cards) divided by gross income
- **Loan-to-value ratio (LTV):** The loan amount divided by the home's value, higher LTV means less risk for the borrower and more risk for the lender
### Rates and refinancing
- **Outstanding Mortgages:** All existing mortgages that haven't been paid off yet
- **Rate Distribution:** How outstanding mortgages are grouped into different buckets by interest rate
- **Refinance Incentive:** A borrower's current rate minus today's market rate. Borrower's need roughly 0.5-0.75 points of saving before refinancing is worth it. 
- **Burnout:** The tendency for refinance waves to weaken over time
- **Mortgage Spread:** The gap between mortgage rates and benchmark Treasury yields 
- **Forward Curve:** The market-implied path of future interest rates
- **Treasury futures and swaps:** Traded contracts whose prices reveal where markets expect interest rates to go
- **Basis Points:** Hundredths of a percentage point (50bp = 0.5%)
### Lender Economics
- **Lender Types:**
	- **Banks:** Depository institutions the make mortgages alongside lending
	- **Independent Mortgage Banks:** Non-bank lenders that focus on mortgages (Rocket)
	- **Credit Unions:** Member-owned depository institutions
- **Production:** The lending industry's term for the business of originating loans
- **Cost per loan, Revenue per loan**
- **Breakeven volume:** The number of closings at which revenue exactly covers costs
- **Fixed vs. Variable Costs**
- **Cost Per Employee**
### Roles in Mortgage Production
**Loan Officer:** The salesperson who works with borrowers, takes applications, and is isually largely on commission 
**Processor:** Collects and organizes the documents
**Underwriter:** Evaluates the file against lending guidelines
### Data Sources and Institutions
- **HMDA (Home Mortgage Disclosure Act) data:** A federal dataset covering nearly every. Mortgage application in the US containing outcome, loan type, purpose, amount, DTI, LTV
- **MBA (Mortgage Bankers Association):** The industry's main trade group, its weekly application survey tracks volumes
- **Freddie Mac PMMS (Primary Mortgage Market Survey):** Freddie Mac's weekly survey of average mortgage rates
- **FHFA National Mortgage Database:** A Federal Housing Finance Agency dataset with aggregate statistics on outstanding mortgages, including the rate distribution used in the refinance model 
- **NAR Existing Home Sales:** The National Association of Realtors' monthly count of sales of previously owned homes
- **NAICS 522292:** Government industry code for mortgage lenders
